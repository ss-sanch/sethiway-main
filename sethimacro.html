Macroeconomic Glossary

2Y/10Y Yield Spread

The difference between the 10-Year and 2-Year US Treasury yields. A "normal" curve slopes upward. When the 2-Year yields more than the 10-Year, the curve is Inverted—historically one of the most reliable warning signs of an impending economic recession on Wall Street.

Sahm Rule (Recession Indicator)

A highly accurate, modern macroeconomic rule developed by former Fed economist Claudia Sahm. It signals the start of a recession when the three-month moving average of the national unemployment rate rises by 0.50% or more relative to its lowest point in the previous 12 months.

Taylor Rule Target

A monetary policy formula that calculates what the "ideal" interest rate should be based on current inflation and GDP/unemployment data. Comparing the Taylor Rule Target to the Actual Fed Funds rate shows whether the central bank is currently being too restrictive or too accommodative.

Actual Fed Funds Rate

The target interest rate set by the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC). It is the foundational "cost of capital" for the entire global economy, directly influencing mortgage rates, corporate debt, and equity valuations (DCF discount rates).

Headline vs. Core Inflation

Headline CPI measures the total inflation across the entire economy. Core PCE strips out highly volatile food and energy prices, making it the preferred, stable metric that the Federal Reserve actually uses to set interest rates.

Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP)

The total number of paid U.S. workers of any business, excluding general government employees, private household employees, and employees of nonprofit organizations. It is the most heavily traded monthly labor statistic on Wall Street, acting as a proxy for raw economic growth.
