Sahm Recession Rule

A highly accurate macroeconomic rule. It signals the start of a recession when the 3-month moving average of the unemployment rate rises by 0.50% relative to its lowest point in the previous 12 months.

Global Yield Spread

The difference between the US 10-Year Treasury yield and the UK 10-Year Gilt. This spread dictates global capital flows. Capital generally flows toward the country offering the higher risk-free return, strengthening their currency.

Fed Policy Gap (Taylor Rule)

Calculates the "ideal" interest rate based on current inflation and unemployment. A negative gap means the Fed is currently Accommodative (stimulating growth), while a positive gap means they are Restrictive (cooling the economy).

Dollar Index (DXY) Momentum

Tracks the rolling 30-day percentage change in the strength of the US Dollar against a basket of foreign currencies. A rising dollar often pressures emerging market debt and multinational corporate earnings.

Global Unemployment

Tracks the percentage of the labor force actively looking for work. Comparing the US, UK, and Eurozone reveals divergence in global economic health and central bank policy timing. A rising trend here is the ultimate leading indicator for interest rate cuts.

Wage Growth (The Spiral)

Measures the year-over-year change in average hourly earnings. This is heavily monitored due to the Wage-Price Spiral: if wages grow too fast, corporations raise product prices to compensate, forcing the Fed to keep interest rates higher for longer to combat sticky inflation.

JOLTS (Job Openings)

The Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey tracks the total number of unfilled jobs in the US. Extremely high openings indicate a "tight" labor market where workers have leverage to demand higher pay. The Fed targets lower JOLTS numbers to safely declare victory over inflation.

Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP)

The holy grail of monthly economic data. It represents the total number of paid workers added to the US economy (excluding farm employees). A massive "beat" above Wall Street consensus can send bond yields soaring, while a severe "miss" instantly sparks recession fears.

Central Bank Rates

Tracks the benchmark interest rates set by the US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank. Divergence here drives global currency flows. If the Fed keeps rates higher than the ECB, capital floods into the US Dollar seeking higher yield.

10Y Sovereign Spreads

Compares the yield on 10-Year government bonds (US Treasuries, UK Gilts, German Bunds). A widening gap between the US and Europe signals stronger US economic growth expectations and acts as a massive leading indicator for global exchange rates.

US Inflation Components

Tracks the deterioration of purchasing power. Headline CPI is what consumers feel. Core PCE strips out volatile food/energy and is the Fed's preferred metric. PPI measures wholesale costs at the factory door, acting as a leading indicator for future CPI spikes.

Real Yields & Breakevens

The ultimate drivers of asset valuation. Breakevens show the market's expected inflation rate over the next 10 years. Real Yields (TIPS) show the true cost of borrowing after adjusting for inflation. When Real Yields surge, tech stocks and gold typically sell off heavily.

Global Real GDP Growth

The broadest measure of economic activity, adjusted for inflation. Tracking the US, UK, and Eurozone side-by-side reveals global economic divergence. A slowing GDP combined with high inflation forces central banks into a difficult "Stagflation" corner.

Industrial Production

Measures the raw output of factories, mines, and utilities in the United States. Because industrial activity is highly cyclical, a sharp contraction here serves as a reliable coincident indicator that a corporate earnings recession is underway.

Retail Sales (The Engine)

Consumer spending accounts for roughly 70% of the entire US GDP. Retail Sales provides a real-time snapshot of the consumer's wallet. If retail spending remains resilient despite high interest rates, the economy typically avoids recession.

Consumer Sentiment

The University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment index is a leading indicator. It doesn't tell us what consumers spent yesterday—it predicts what they will spend tomorrow. Plummeting sentiment often precedes a severe pullback in retail sales and broader economic contraction.

US Yield Curve (Term Structure)

Displays government bond yields across time horizons. A normal curve slopes upward. An inverted or shifting curve indicates rapidly changing expectations for growth and Fed policy.

10Y-2Y Yield Spread

Tracks the difference between the 10-Year and 2-Year Treasury yields. When the spread drops below the 0.0% zero-line, the curve is inverted—Wall Street's most closely watched recession indicator.

Macro Risk Barometer (0–100)

A composite quantitative indicator synthesizing three distinct macroeconomic vulnerabilities into a single systemic score:

  • Labor Fragility (40%): Sensitivity to Sahm Rule acceleration.
  • Term Structure Stress (35%): Inversion depth of the 10Y–2Y spread.
  • Monetary Restrictiveness (25%): Taylor Rule policy rate gap.

Scores above 65 signal elevated recession probability and systemic market stress.
