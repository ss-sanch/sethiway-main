Black-Scholes Parameters

  • Underlying Price ($S$): The current market price of the asset.
  • Strike Price ($K$): The predetermined price at which the option can be exercised.
  • Days to Expiry ($T$): Time remaining until the contract expires. (The engine automatically converts this to an annualized decimal).
  • Implied Volatility ($\sigma$): The market's theoretical forecast of how much the security's price will move. Higher IV directly inflates option premiums.
  • Risk-Free Rate ($r$): The theoretical baseline return of an investment with zero risk, typically pegged to the US 10-Year Treasury Yield.

The Greeks (Risk Matrix)

  • Delta ($\Delta$): Directional risk. Estimates how much the option's price will change for every $1 move in the underlying asset.
  • Gamma ($\Gamma$): Convexity risk. The rate of change in Delta for a $1 move in the underlying. Highest for at-the-money options.
  • Theta ($\Theta$): Time decay. Measures how much theoretical value the option loses each day simply as time passes, assuming all else is equal.
  • Vega ($\mathcal{V}$): Volatility risk. How much the option's premium will adjust for a 1% change in Implied Volatility.
