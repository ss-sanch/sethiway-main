Risk Profile (VaR & Volatility)

Value at Risk (VaR): A statistical technique used on Wall Street to quantify financial risk. A 95% VaR of -3.5% means we are 95% confident the stock will not lose more than 3.5% of its value in a single trading day, based on 5 years of historical data.

Volatility: The annualized standard deviation of daily returns. Higher percentages indicate a wider swing in daily price action.

Advanced Valuation Engine

This engine uses distinct mathematical models to calculate a range of intrinsic values for the target company, creating a comprehensive "Football Field" of potential target entry prices.

Macro-Pegged Discount Rate: Your baseline discount rate is dynamically tethered to the live US 10-Year Real Treasury Yield via the SethiMacro terminal, plus a standard 5.0% Equity Risk Premium. This ensures your valuation automatically adapts to today's live macroeconomic risk environment.

Exit Multiple DCF: Calculates terminal value based on a multiple of the final year's cash flow. Best for established, steady-state businesses.

Gordon Growth DCF: Assumes the company will grow cash flows perpetually at a stable rate. Highly sensitive to the Perpetual Growth Rate input. If the discount rate is lower than the growth rate, this model mathematically breaks and returns N/A.

DuPont ROE Analysis

Return on Equity (ROE) is a massive profitability indicator, but it can be manipulated with debt. The DuPont model breaks ROE down into its three true drivers:

  • Net Margin: Operating efficiency (Are they pricing their product well?)
  • Asset Turnover: Asset use efficiency (Are they selling enough product?)
  • Equity Multiplier: Financial leverage (Are they pumping up returns using dangerous debt?)

The Sethi Score

The Sethi Score is a proprietary algorithmic grading system out of 100. It quickly evaluates a stock's fundamental health based on 10 strict Wall Street financial metrics.

  • Positive Net Income: Company is currently profitable.
  • Consistent Revenue Growth: Revenue has increased year-over-year.
  • Positive Free Cash Flow: Generating real cash after capital expenditures.
  • Return on Equity (ROE) > 15%: Highly efficient at generating profits from shareholder equity.
  • Net Profit Margin > 10%: Keeping a healthy percentage of total revenue as profit.
  • Debt-to-Equity Ratio < 1.0: Conservative leverage with manageable debt levels.
  • Free Cash Flow Yield > 5%: Attractive cash generation relative to its valuation.
  • P/E Ratio < 25: Trading at a reasonable valuation compared to its earnings.
  • P/B Ratio < 5: Trading at a reasonable valuation relative to its book value.
  • Pays a Dividend: Returns cash directly to shareholders.

Quantitative Terminal Glossary

Value & Profitability

  • Market Cap: The total market value of the company's outstanding shares.
  • EV/EBITDA: Compares a company's total value (including debt) to its raw cash earnings. The gold standard of valuation.
  • P/E Ratio: Price-to-Earnings. How much investors are willing to pay per dollar of earnings.
  • ROE (Return on Equity): Measures how efficiently the company turns shareholder cash into profits.
  • EPS (TTM): Earnings Per Share. The portion of a company's profit allocated to each outstanding share over the last 12 months.
  • Forward EPS: Wall Street analysts' consensus estimate of what the company's Earnings Per Share will be over the next 12 months.

Risk & Yield

  • Beta: A measure of volatility. A Beta > 1 means the stock is more volatile than the S&P 500; < 1 means it is less volatile.
  • Short Interest: The percentage of available shares that are being shorted by hedge funds betting the price will fall.
  • Dividend Yield: How much the company pays out in dividends each year relative to its share price.
  • FCF Yield: Free Cash Flow Yield. High numbers indicate the company is generating massive cash relative to its valuation.

Technicals & Momentum

  • 14d RSI: Relative Strength Index. A momentum oscillator ranging from 0-100. >70 is historically "Overbought", <30 is "Oversold".
  • Stochastic: Compares a particular closing price to a range of its prices over a period of time to predict trend reversals.
  • % From 200MA: How far the current price has strayed from its 200-day Simple Moving Average (the baseline trend of the stock).
  • % From 52W High: Shows immediate upside required to break out into new yearly highs.

Catalysts

  • Next Earnings: The estimated date the company will report quarterly financials, driving major price volatility.
  • Ex-Dividend: You must own the stock before this date to receive the upcoming dividend payout.

The Mini-LBO Model

A Leveraged Buyout (LBO) is the acquisition of another company using a significant amount of borrowed money (debt) to meet the cost of acquisition. The assets of the company being acquired are often used as collateral for the loans.

This calculator executes a "Paper LBO" (a rapid, back-of-the-envelope model heavily utilized in Private Equity interview technical tests). While institutional PE models contain hundreds of rows for working capital, tax shields, and multi-tranche debt schedules, the fundamental drivers of returns are simplified here to four core levers:

  • Entry/Exit Multiple: What the sponsor pays for the business versus what they sell it for, typically mapped to EV/EBITDA.
  • Debt Funding %: How much of the purchase price is funded by the bank. More debt means less sponsor equity is required, which amplifies returns (if the company performs well).
  • Interest Rate: The cost of the debt. The company's Free Cash Flow must be high enough to pay this off over the holding period.
  • Holding Period: Typically 3 to 7 years. The faster the sponsor can grow the company and pay down the debt, the higher the annualized return.

Target PE Returns Private Equity firms typically target a minimum of >20% IRR (Internal Rate of Return) and a >2.0x MoM (Multiple on Money) over a standard 5-year holding period.
