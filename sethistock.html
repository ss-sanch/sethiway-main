The Sethi Score

The Sethi Score is a proprietary algorithmic grading system out of 100. It quickly evaluates a stock's fundamental health based on 10 strict Wall Street financial metrics.

  • Positive Net Income: Company is currently profitable.
  • Consistent Revenue Growth: Revenue has increased year-over-year.
  • Positive Free Cash Flow: Generating real cash after capital expenditures.
  • Return on Equity (ROE) > 15%: Highly efficient at generating profits from shareholder equity.
  • Net Profit Margin > 10%: Keeping a healthy percentage of total revenue as profit.
  • Debt-to-Equity Ratio < 1.0: Conservative leverage with manageable debt levels.
  • Free Cash Flow Yield > 5%: Attractive cash generation relative to its valuation.
  • P/E Ratio < 25: Trading at a reasonable valuation compared to its earnings.
  • P/B Ratio < 5: Trading at a reasonable valuation relative to its book value.
  • Pays a Dividend: Returns cash directly to shareholders.

Understanding Core Metrics

Market Cap: The total market value of a company's outstanding shares. It helps categorise companies by size and risk profile.

EV/EBITDA: Enterprise Value to Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation. A comprehensive valuation metric that compares a company's total value (including debt) to its cash earnings.

P/E Ratio (TTM): Price-to-Earnings Ratio. Compares the current share price to its per-share earnings. Used to gauge if a stock is over or undervalued relative to its peers.

P/B Ratio (TTM): Price-to-Book Ratio. Compares the market valuation to the company's actual net asset value (book value).

EPS (TTM): Earnings Per Share. The portion of a company's profit allocated to each outstanding share.

Div Yield: The financial ratio that shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its share price.

FCF Yield: Free Cash Flow Yield. A high yield often indicates a company is generating plenty of cash relative to its valuation.

ROE: Return on Equity. A massive indicator of efficiency, showing how much profit a company generates with the money shareholders have invested.

95% VaR (5Y): Value at Risk. A Wall Street statistical technique. A 95% VaR of -3.5% means we are 95% confident the stock will not lose more than 3.5% of its value in a single trading day, based on 5 years of historical data.

Volatility: The annualized standard deviation of daily returns. Higher percentages indicate a wider, riskier swing in daily price action.

Advanced Valuation Engine

This engine uses two separate mathematical models to calculate a range of intrinsic values for the target company, creating a comprehensive "Football Field" of potential target entry prices.

Exit Multiple DCF: Calculates terminal value based on a multiple of the final year's cash flow. Best for established, steady-state businesses.

Gordon Growth DCF: Assumes the company will grow cash flows perpetually at a stable rate. Highly sensitive to the Perpetual Growth Rate input. If the discount rate is lower than the growth rate, this model mathematically breaks and returns N/A.

The Valuation Football Field

The Football Field chart visually plots the intrinsic price targets generated by our valuation models against the stock's actual 52-Week trading range.

How to read it: If the current price (the dotted red line) falls to the left of the colored valuation bars, the stock is currently trading below its theoretical fair value, indicating a potential margin of safety.
